# What does an outsourced back office cost in Australia?

There's no single price for an outsourced back office, and anyone who quotes you one before asking what you need is guessing. What you can know is what drives the cost, what the alternative really costs, and what to ask so you can compare quotes fairly. This guide covers all three, and explains how Me² quotes.

We don't publish Me² prices. Every quote is built around what your business needs, and you'll know the price before anything starts.

## First, what does a local hire cost?

Most owners compare outsourcing with hiring someone. The wage is the obvious part, but it's not the whole cost.

**The wage.** Many admin and office roles fall under the Clerks – Private Sector Award. From 1 July 2025, the minimum full-time rate for a Level 2 (year 1) adult employee was $1,068.40 a week, or $28.12 an hour.[^1] From the first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026, award minimums rose by a further 4.75%.[^2] Experienced bookkeepers, customer service leads and marketers are usually paid well above award minimums. [CONFIRM: current 2026-27 rate]

**Super.** Employers must pay super of 12% of ordinary time earnings from 1 July 2025.[^3]

**Leave.** Full-time employees get four weeks' paid annual leave and 10 days' paid personal/carer's leave a year under the National Employment Standards.[^4] When your admin person is on leave, the work either waits or lands on someone else.

**Payroll tax and workers' compensation.** Payroll tax depends on your state and your total wages bill, and each state sets its own threshold. Workers' compensation insurance is compulsory for employers. [Link to state revenue office in final copy]

**The costs that don't show on a payslip.** Recruitment (advertising, time spent interviewing), a desk, a computer and software licences, training, and the management time someone spends checking the work. And if the person leaves, you pay most of these again, often after the process has walked out the door with them.

None of this means hiring is the wrong choice. A good local hire who stays for years is valuable. It means the fair comparison is total cost and total risk, not hourly rate against hourly rate.

## The three main ways to outsource

**1. Freelancers through a marketplace.** You find, brief and manage the person yourself, and pay by the hour or the job, plus any platform fees. Lowest commitment, but you're the manager, the trainer and the quality check. See [Me² vs hiring a freelance virtual assistant](/compare/virtual-assistant-marketplaces/).

**2. Dedicated offshore staff through a staffing provider.** The provider recruits a person who works your hours, and you direct their work day to day. These providers often publish hourly rates. One Philippines-based provider, for example, lists rates starting from A$8 an hour for a virtual assistant and from A$11 an hour for a bookkeeper, for 20 to 40 hours a week.[^5] Starting rates are a floor, not a quote, and what you pay depends on the person's skills and experience.

**3. A managed back office.** You hand over outcomes (invoices chased, inbox cleared, books ready for your accountant) rather than hours. The provider supplies the people, writes the processes, checks the work and answers for it. This is how Me² works.

The models suit different businesses. If you have a clear job and time to manage someone, a dedicated staff member can work well. If you want the work done and checked without becoming a manager of offshore staff, a managed service is usually the better fit.

## What drives the cost

Whatever the model, these are the things that move a quote up or down:

- **Volume.** How many invoices, emails, bookings or posts a week. This matters more than the number of task types.
- **Skill and specialism.** Data entry costs less than reconciliations; reconciliations cost less than paid-ads management. Work that needs a specialist costs more than general admin.
- **Coverage.** Business hours only, or evenings and weekends too.
- **How much you want to manage.** Paying for hours is cheaper on paper if your own time is free. It rarely is.
- **Checking and accountability.** Who checks the work before it reaches your customers, and who fixes it when it's wrong.
- **Set-up.** Writing down your processes, setting up access and tools, and any AI agents built around your business.
- **Your tools.** Working in the software you already use costs less than migrating to new systems.
- **Data and security needs.** Sensitive data needs tighter access controls. See our [guide to outsourcing bookkeeping and admin safely](/guides/offshore-data-privacy/).
- **Terms.** Minimum hours, minimum terms and notice periods all change what you're really committing to.

## Questions to ask any provider

Use these to compare quotes like for like:

1. Is the price per hour, per month or per job? What exactly is included?
2. Are there recruitment, set-up, equipment or replacement fees?
3. Are there minimum hours or a minimum term? What's the notice period?
4. Who manages the person day to day: you or the provider?
5. Who checks the work before it goes out, and what happens when something is wrong?
6. If the person leaves or is sick, what happens to the work?
7. Who owns the written processes, logins and data if you leave?
8. Who can see your data, and how is their access removed?
9. Is GST included? Are there card surcharges?
10. How quickly can they start?

## The short version

An outsourced back office can cost less than a local hire, but the honest comparison includes on-costs, leave cover, management time and the cost of the work being wrong. Get quotes that spell out what's included, who checks the work and how you leave, then compare those, not the hourly rate.
