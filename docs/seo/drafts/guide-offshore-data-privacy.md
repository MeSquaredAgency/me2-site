# Is it safe to outsource bookkeeping and admin offshore?

It can be, if you and your provider do the work to make it safe. Handing someone your accounting login, your inbox and your customers' details takes trust, and trust should rest on things you can check: who can see your data, how they get access, how it's taken away, and what the law expects of you.

This guide covers the Australian rules in plain English, the controls to insist on from any provider, and how Me² handles your data. It isn't legal advice. If your business handles health information or other sensitive data, talk to your lawyer before outsourcing.

## Does the Privacy Act apply to you?

The Privacy Act 1988 and its 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply to businesses with an annual turnover of more than $3 million, and to some smaller businesses, including private health service providers and businesses that buy or sell personal information.[^oaic-who]

Even if your business is under the threshold, the APPs are a good standard to hold a provider to. Your customers don't know your turnover; they just expect their details to be looked after.

## What APP 8 asks of you

APP 8 covers sending personal information overseas. In the OAIC's words, giving personal information to an overseas contractor is "in most circumstances" a disclosure.[^app8-812] Before you disclose it, you need to take reasonable steps to make sure the overseas recipient doesn't breach the APPs. The OAIC says it generally expects that to include an enforceable contract.[^app8-816]

There's also a catch many owners don't know: under section 16C of the Privacy Act, you can be held accountable for what the overseas recipient does with that information, even if you took reasonable steps.[^app8-860] There are exceptions, such as where the recipient is bound by a law substantially similar to the APPs, but you shouldn't assume one applies without advice.

In practice, that means three things:

1. **Have a written agreement** that says how the provider will handle personal information, consistent with the APPs.
2. **Choose a provider whose controls you can check**, because their mistakes can become yours.
3. **Tell your customers.** Your privacy policy should say that personal information may be handled overseas, and where.[^app1]

## The controls to insist on

Whoever you outsource to, in any country, ask for these:

**Named people, individual logins.** Each person working on your business should have their own login, never a shared password. That way you can see who did what, and remove one person without changing everything.

**Least access.** People should only have access to the systems their job needs. The person chasing invoices doesn't need your payroll.

**Multi-factor authentication** on every account that supports it, especially your accounting software, bank feeds and email.

**Access removed when the work ends**, and a clear answer on how fast.

**No data used to train AI.** If the provider uses AI tools, ask whether your data is used to train AI models, and get the answer in writing.

**Approval limits.** Payments, refunds and anything legal should need your approval. Write the limits down.

**A plan for when something goes wrong.** Under the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme, an eligible data breach must be reported to the OAIC and the people affected.[^ndb] Ask how the provider would tell you, and how quickly. [CONFIRM: Me²'s breach process]

**You keep ownership.** Your data, your logins and the processes written for you should stay yours if you leave.

## Bookkeeping in particular

For bookkeeping, a few extras help:

- Add your provider as a **user** in your accounting software with the right role, rather than sharing the owner's login. Most accounting packages let you set user roles and restrict access to payroll or bank details.
- Keep **payments with you**. Let the provider prepare payment runs and code bills; you approve and release them.
- Keep **tax lodgement with your registered tax or BAS agent**, who can review the work your provider prepares.
