# Cambodia or the Philippines? A fair comparison for Australian businesses

If you've looked into offshore help for your back office, you've almost certainly come across Philippines-based providers. The Philippines is the best-known outsourcing destination for Australian businesses, and for good reason. Cambodia is a smaller, newer option.

Me²'s team is in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, so we have a stake in this. We've tried to write the guide we'd want to read: sourced, fair to both countries, and clear about where each one fits. Where the facts favour the Philippines, we say so.

## At a glance

| | Philippines | Cambodia |
|---|---|---|
| Time zone | UTC+8, no daylight saving | UTC+7, no daylight saving |
| Behind Sydney/Melbourne (AEST / AEDT) | 2 hours / 3 hours | 3 hours / 4 hours |
| English | An official language; high proficiency in the EF index | Not an official language; low national proficiency in the EF index, higher in Phnom Penh |
| Outsourcing industry | Large and mature: about 1.9 million IT-BPM workers | Small and young, concentrated in Phnom Penh |
| Data protection law | Data Privacy Act of 2012, with a national regulator | Comprehensive law still in draft |

The sources for each row are below.

## Time zones

The Philippines is on UTC+8 and Cambodia on UTC+7. Neither has daylight saving.[^tz] Against the eastern states:

- **During AEST (April to October):** Manila is 2 hours behind Sydney and Melbourne; Phnom Penh is 3 hours behind.
- **During AEDT (October to April):** Manila is 3 hours behind; Phnom Penh is 4 hours behind.[^dst]

Queensland doesn't observe daylight saving, so it stays 2 and 3 hours ahead of Manila and Phnom Penh all year. Perth (UTC+8) shares Manila's time and is 1 hour ahead of Phnom Penh.

**What it means in practice.** A 9 am start in Sydney is 7 am in Manila and 6 am in Phnom Penh during AEST, and an hour earlier again in summer. For full morning overlap with the east coast, the Philippines is easier. Cambodian teams that cover Australian mornings work early starts; Me²'s roles, for example, list "a 6:00 AM start where required to align with Australian business hours".[^me2hours]

The other side of the same coin: a 7 pm enquiry in Melbourne in summer arrives at 3 pm in Phnom Penh, inside a normal working day. If evening and weekend replies matter to you more than 8 am overlap, a later time zone helps.

## English

This is the Philippines' clearest advantage.

- English is an official language of the Philippines, alongside Filipino.[^ph-const] In the 2025 EF English Proficiency Index, the Philippines ranked 28th of 123 countries and regions with a score of 569, in the "high" band, and second in Asia.[^ef-ph]
- Cambodia ranked 123rd with a score of 390. Phnom Penh scored 472, well above the national figure.[^ef-kh]

Two caveats. EF's index is built from people who chose to take its free online test, and EF itself says the sample may not be representative.[^ef-method] And a national average says little about the person who will do your work. What matters is the English of the specific people a provider hires, and how it's tested.

One industry guide describes Cambodia's English-speaking pool as small, with written English serviceable among graduates but spoken fluency less widespread, and says voice work for Western customers is rarely a good fit.[^oa]

**What it means in practice.** If you need phone-based customer service at scale, the Philippines is the safer choice. For written work (email, messages, documents, data and reports) a Cambodian provider that hires carefully for written English, and checks the work, can do the job well. Ask any provider, in either country, how they test English and who checks what goes to your customers. [CONFIRM: Me²'s English standard/test]

## Talent market and maturity

The Philippine IT-BPM industry is one of the largest in the world. Its industry association, IBPAP, reported about 1.9 million workers and more than US$40 billion in revenue in 2025.[^ibpap] That means a deep pool of people with outsourcing experience, established providers, and specialists in most back-office fields, including Australian bookkeeping and property management.

Cambodia's industry is much smaller and younger. One industry guide notes that "a hundred people is a substantial Cambodian operation", that senior technical talent is scarce, and that provider track records are shorter than in mature markets.[^oa] We found no current official figure for Cambodia's outsourcing workforce. [CONFIRM: any CDC or government figure]

**What it means in practice.** If you need a large team quickly, or a hard-to-find specialist, the Philippines has more depth. If you need a small, well-run team and care more about who is running it than the size of the market, Cambodia can work, but look harder at the provider's own training, processes and checking.

## Costs

Comparing countries on cost is harder than it looks, and most published comparisons quote minimum wages that have little to do with office work.

For reference only:

- In Metro Manila, the non-agricultural minimum wage rose to ₱695 a day from 18 July 2025.[^ncr]
- In Cambodia, the minimum wage set for the garment, textile and footwear sectors is US$210 a month from 1 January 2026.[^kh-wage]

Neither is a guide to what a skilled bookkeeper or customer service specialist earns, and what you pay a provider includes management, training, equipment, checking and margin, not just a wage. Philippines-based providers often publish starting hourly rates, so it's easy to get a feel for that market. Cambodian providers usually quote. [CONFIRM: wording]

**What it means in practice.** Don't choose a country on wage tables. Compare the total monthly cost of getting the work done to the standard you need, including your own management time. See [what an outsourced back office costs in Australia](/guides/outsourced-back-office-cost/).

## Data and privacy

If your business is covered by the Australian Privacy Act, sending personal information to an overseas provider usually counts as a disclosure, and Australian Privacy Principle 8 asks you to take reasonable steps to make sure the recipient handles it in line with the APPs. In most cases you also stay accountable for what the overseas provider does with it.[^app8] That's true for both countries.

The difference is local law:

- **The Philippines** has had a comprehensive Data Privacy Act since 2012 (Republic Act 10173), with a National Privacy Commission to enforce it.[^ph-dpa]
- **Cambodia** has no comprehensive personal data protection law in force yet. A draft was released in 2025; as of February 2026 it had not been enacted.[^kh-dpa] [CONFIRM status on publishing]

**What it means in practice.** With a Cambodian provider, your contract and the provider's own controls carry more of the weight: who can see your data, how logins are issued and removed, whether your data is used to train AI, and what happens if something goes wrong. Ask for those in writing. In either country, you can't rely on local law alone to meet your APP 8 obligations. Our guide on [outsourcing bookkeeping and admin safely](/guides/offshore-data-privacy/) goes through the questions.

## When each fits

**The Philippines is likely the better fit if:**

- you need phone-based customer service, especially at volume
- you want a large team, or to scale quickly
- you want a provider with a long track record, or a niche specialist
- you want the most overlap with east-coast mornings

**Cambodia can be the better fit if:**

- the work is mostly written: email, messages, documents, data, bookkeeping support, reports
- you want a small team that's managed and checked for you, rather than staff you direct yourself
- evening replies across Australian time zones matter to you
- the provider can show you, in writing, how it hires, trains, checks work and protects data

Whichever country you choose, the provider matters more than the map.
